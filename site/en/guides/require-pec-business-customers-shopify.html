Require PEC from business customers at Shopify checkout

To require PEC from business customers at Shopify checkout, you can ask for it only from customers who fill in Company and keep it optional for everyone else. The PEC field sits in Shopify’s Italian checkout, next to Codice Fiscale.

Updated 14 November 2026

The native PEC field

PEC, Italy’s certified email, uses one of Shopify’s native tax fields, separate from the order email and from Codice Fiscale. Shopify decides when to show it at checkout: CF Ready does not create it, add it to the theme or repurpose Address 2. In the standard setup the field is optional, and customers can complete the order with it empty.

CF Ready adds a rule on top of this field, run by Shopify’s Validation during checkout. The PEC rule is independent from the Codice Fiscale rule.

Four PEC modes

On the app’s Checkout rules page you choose one of these modes:

  • Not managed: CF Ready does not check the field and checkout stays as it is today.
  • Optional and validated: customers can leave it empty; if they fill it in, it must have the form of a valid email address.
  • Required and validated: customers cannot complete the order without an address in a valid form.
  • Required when Company is filled in: PEC is needed only from customers who enter a company in the billing address.

Codice Fiscale has the first three modes. You can combine them as you like: for example Codice Fiscale required for everyone and PEC required only from businesses.

How required PEC works with the Company field

CF Ready reads the Company field of the billing address and ignores leading and trailing spaces. If any text remains, PEC becomes required: an empty field blocks order confirmation with the message you chose. If Company is empty, PEC stays optional.

In both cases a PEC that is filled in is always checked for its form. The general rules still apply: checks cover deliveries in Italy and do not apply when the billing address is outside Italy.

Company must appear at checkout.

If customers do not see or fill in Company in the billing address, PEC stays optional. Before you activate the rule, complete a test checkout and check where the Company field appears in your store.

CF Ready does not infer the tax treatment of the order from the Company field. It uses that field only as a signal to ask for PEC. You decide with your accountant whether and when to collect it.

What CF Ready checks on PEC

The check is formal. CF Ready verifies that the address has exactly one at sign, a non-empty local part and domain, no internal spaces and a correctly written domain, subdomains included. Upper and lower case are both accepted.

When the value is rejected, customers see your message followed by a short sentence explaining the formal cause, so they can fix it without guessing.

CF Ready does not query the domain, does not use lists of PEC providers and does not check that the mailbox exists. A well-formed address is not necessarily an active PEC mailbox.

Examples of accepted and rejected PEC addresses

These examples use placeholder domains. They show what passes the formal check and what hint customers read when the value is rejected.

  • amministrazione@pec.example.it: accepted.
  • Ordini.Rossi@PEC.Example.it: accepted, because upper and lower case don’t matter. With order standardization it is saved in lowercase.
  • amministrazione@example.it: accepted. The check is formal, so a well-written ordinary email also passes.
  • amministrazione pec@example.it: rejected, it contains a space. Leading and trailing spaces, on the other hand, don’t count.
  • amministrazione.pec.example.it: rejected, the at sign is missing.
  • .amministrazione@pec.example.it: rejected, the text before the at sign cannot start or end with a dot.
  • amministrazione@pec: rejected, the domain needs at least one dot, as in pec.example.it.
  • amministrazione@pec..example.it: rejected, the domain contains two consecutive dots.

If your store often receives ordinary emails instead of a PEC, say so in the message you choose for the field, for example by reminding customers that the company’s PEC address is needed.

What stays out of scope

CF Ready handles only Codice Fiscale and PEC. It does not handle VAT numbers or SDI recipient codes, and it does not issue invoices or send them to SDI. If you need that data, look at the invoicing tools you already use.

How to require PEC from business customers

  1. Open Checkout rules and choose the PEC mode.
  2. Choose the Codice Fiscale mode separately.
  3. In the simulator, try “Company filled in without PEC” and a badly written PEC.
  4. Save the rules, then activate the Validation: they are two separate actions.
  5. Complete a test checkout with and without Company.

After the order you find PEC together with the Codice Fiscale on the order details page. If you want consistent addresses, the app can save it in lowercase in orders.

Frequently asked questions

Does this rule make PEC required for every customer?

No. With “Required when Company is filled in”, PEC is required only if the customer fills in Company in the billing address. Otherwise it stays optional and is checked when entered.

Does CF Ready check that the PEC mailbox exists?

No. It checks that the address is written in a valid form. It does not query the domain, does not use lists of PEC providers and does not confirm that the mailbox is active or registered as PEC.

Can I require PEC without requiring Codice Fiscale?

Yes. Codice Fiscale and PEC have independent rules: you can leave Codice Fiscale unmanaged or optional and require PEC, or the other way round.

Is PEC required when billing is outside Italy?

No. With a billing address outside Italy CF Ready applies no rules, even if Company is filled in and delivery is in Italy.
